## Whole-paper assessment

The paper studies a concrete supervisory-control failure: a clear waypoint chord can induce an unsafe executed trajectory under a fixed recurrent sailing controller. The proposed supervisor evaluates finite reference-and-hold protocols using a separate instance of the inherited policy and dynamics, causal history reconstruction and a locally fitted wind vector. Its nominal-first guard and next-step delivery timing are explicit. This is a control-engineering integration and empirical diagnosis, with modest novelty, rather than a new learned controller or safety theorem.

The evidence supports publication within the declared ideal-compute, noiseless, single constant-velocity target simulation scope. The main comparison contains 24 frozen tasks and 72 executions, with safe completion counts 19/24 for reactive navigation, 23/24 for the calibrated low-order predictor and 24/24 for detailed prediction. The strongest reasons to retain the paper are the execution-aware formulation, the meaningful low-order comparator, the audited timing/history implementation and the unusually explicit limits on the benefit. Detailed prediction buys one additional completion over the low-order comparator; it does not dominate efficiency. The manuscript preserves the common-safe mean cost of +7 s, the +308 s adverse detour, the approximately 106 m adverse prediction error and substantial computation cost. It does not convert the three integration-substep replays into additional samples or claim real-time safety.

The prediction-clearance comparison uses a common time/obstacle domain, and the corrected minimum limits and notation remain readable in the final PDF. The primary scene envelope and pressure-family confounding remain disclosed. No new material formula, causal-information, execution or result-to-claim defect was found in this version. I do not require a new network, theorem, physical-vessel campaign, full COLREGs system or general population superiority for this bounded contribution.
