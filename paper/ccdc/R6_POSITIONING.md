# R6 paper positioning update (R3 draft preserved)

Proposed problem: continuous navigation without direct wind sensing under a known static map, using available learned sail/rudder controllers whose local-task interfaces need adaptation. Frozen low-level control is no longer the research premise. The selected checkpoint happened to remain fixed during R6; this is an implementation choice and fair ablation condition, not an unavoidable problem restriction.

Selected system: an inherited SAC-LSTM-attention policy, rigid goal-frame/history reconstruction, known-map safety-boundary distance encoding, and the existing simple Relay120 planner. Attribute SAC to Haarnoja et al. (ICML 2018), scaled dot-product attention to Vaswani et al. (2017), and the policy/training assets to the upstream work identified in R6_ASSETS.json. Do not claim a new network, SAC variant, safety theorem or exact replication of the source FOV protocol. Required asset authorship/release permissions remain an author responsibility before publication.

Evidence: 54 frozen new synthetic instances, 1/3/5 km routine plus a separately defined risk family. The selected package safely completed 54/54; original Relay120 52/54, Relay160 51/54, prior recovery 53/54. With the same 18D checkpoint and Relay120, center-distance and raw-zero obstacle inputs each completed 51/54. This isolates the aggregate input adaptation, not architecture/training-budget effects. Relative to Relay160, conditional common-safe mean time improved 177.5 s (n=51); relative to Relay120 the median ratio is 1.007, and a 5 km case is 137 s slower. A seen development buffer failure and up to 1.975 s reconstruction delay must remain visible. No newly trained model or training superiority claim.

The defensible contribution is a transparent application/compatibility study with a simple working adaptation and controlled closed-loop evidence. Focus the paper on what changes the selected controller's execution, preserve strong simple controls, and keep negative cases.54/54 is an observation on a limited synthetic sample, not zero failure probability or real-time/real-world validation. R3 draft.md is unchanged. Recommend author-led substantive revision around this evidence, not automatic submission or claims of acceptance readiness.
